# 401 — Hot Mix Asphalt Pavement

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 400 — Pavements
**Section:** 401

---

## Summary

Section 401 governs the furnishing and placement of Hot Mix Asphalt (HMA) pavement on a prepared surface. It establishes material requirements (asphalt grade, aggregate gradation, RAP limits), mix design criteria, submittal requirements, construction procedures, and quality control standards. HMA pavement is the most common pavement type on HDOT highway projects — from overlays and resurfacing to full-depth pavement construction. Key compliance areas include the job-mix formula submittal process, weather and temperature placement restrictions, compaction requirements, and the mandatory use of a Material Transfer Vehicle (MTV) on surface course paving for projects over 1,000 tons.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| HMA | Hot Mix Asphalt — plant-mixed combination of aggregate, asphalt cement, and optionally RAP |
| PG 64-16 | Performance Grade designation for asphalt cement (high temp 64°C, low temp -16°C) — standard for Hawaii |
| RAP | Reclaimed Asphalt Pavement — reprocessed material from removed pavement; allowed up to 15% by weight in surface and binder courses |
| Job-Mix Formula (JMF) | Contractor-designed mix specification establishing aggregate gradation, asphalt content, RAP proportion, and mixing temperature |
| Marshall Method | Mix design method per AASHTO T 245: 75-blow compaction; min 1,800-lb stability; 3–5% air voids |
| Hveem Method | Alternative mix design per AASHTO T 246/247; min stability 37; 3–5% air voids |
| Mix IV / Mix V | Two HMA mix types differentiated by lift thickness and asphalt content ranges |
| Surface Course | Uppermost HMA layer; tighter gradation and quality requirements than binder course |
| Binder Course | Intermediate HMA layer(s) below surface course |
| MTV | Material Transfer Vehicle — independently transfers HMA from trucks to paver; required for surface courses >1,000 tons |
| VMA | Voids in Mineral Aggregate — minimum value depends on nominal max particle size; ranges from 11–15% |

## QRSE Notes

- **JMF must be accepted before production begins.** If a Contractor starts laying HMA before the Engineer accepts the JMF, the work can be ordered removed. Verify JMF submittal status at pre-construction.
- **The 15% RAP limit is a hard ceiling.** Some contractors push for higher RAP content for cost savings. The spec does not allow it for surface and binder courses without separate Engineer acceptance in writing.
- **MTV is mandatory on projects >1,000 tons surface course paving (except Lanai).** This is frequently overlooked on pre-construction submittals. Confirm the Contractor has an MTV in their equipment submittal if the project qualifies. Projects that skip the MTV without justification are non-compliant.
- **Temperature profile measurement** is a QC check — if two consecutive profiles fail the 10°F differential, paving must halt. Inspectors should have non-contact thermometers and know the procedure.
- **Weather restrictions are real.** Hawaii's evening temperatures during certain seasons can drop below 50°F, particularly on elevated roadways. Night paving requires monitoring.
- **Tack coat is required** between existing pavement and new HMA — per Section 407. No tack = bond failure risk. Inspectors should confirm tack has been applied and has cured (broken) before the overlay begins.

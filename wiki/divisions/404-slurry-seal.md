# 404 — Slurry Seal

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 400 — Pavements
**Section:** 404

---

## Summary

Section 404 covers furnishing and applying slurry seal on an existing asphalt surface. Slurry seal is a preventative maintenance treatment — a mixture of emulsified asphalt, fine aggregate, water, and filler applied as a thin surface coating to restore surface texture, fill minor cracks, and extend pavement life. It is not a structural repair method. The section requires a job-mix formula submittal, a test section before production, and strict weather restrictions. Slurry seal is common on HDOT resurfacing and preservation projects and typically lower in cost than an HMA overlay, making it a frequent contract item on pavement maintenance work.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Slurry Seal | Thin surface treatment of emulsified asphalt + fine aggregate + water (+ filler if needed); applied to existing asphalt |
| Emulsified Asphalt (SS-1h / CSS-1h) | Asphalt emulsion types used in slurry seal; CSS-1h is cationic |
| Residual Asphalt Content | Amount of asphalt remaining after the emulsion breaks (water evaporates); ±1% tolerance from accepted JMF |
| Type I / II / III Slurry Seal | ASTM D 3910 classification by aggregate gradation; Type I = finest, Type III = coarsest |
| Test Section | Required 10-foot × 50-foot trial application before full production to verify mix and equipment |
| Job-Mix Formula | Contractor's designed proportioning of aggregate, emulsified asphalt, water, and filler |

## Spec References

- **404.01** — Description: furnishing and applying slurry seal on existing asphalt surface
- **404.02(A)** — Materials: emulsified asphalt (SS-1h or CSS-1h), aggregate, water, filler if required
- **404.02(B)** — JMF design per ASTM D 3910 for Type I, II, or III; ±1% residual asphalt tolerance
- **404.02(C)** — Submittals: JMF must include aggregate gradation, residual asphalt %, aggregate source, emulsion grade, and test data; resubmit if mix is modified after acceptance
- **404.03(A)** — Test section: required before production and after equipment calibration; minimum 10' × 50'
- **404.03(B)** — Weather limitations: no application on wet surfaces; no application below 60°F and falling; may apply above 50°F and rising; not in adverse weather conditions
- **404.03(C)** — Equipment: self-propelled machine specifically designed for slurry seal; continuous-run or truck-mounted; individual volume/weight controls for each component

## QRSE Notes

- **Slurry seal is preventative maintenance, not structural repair.** It should not be specified on pavement that has base failures, alligator cracking, or significant rutting — those require milling and overlay or full-depth reclamation. Specifying slurry seal on structurally deficient pavement is a waste of money.
- **The JMF must be accepted before production** — same principle as HMA. No approved JMF = no production. Confirm this at pre-construction.
- **Test section is mandatory.** This is often skipped on smaller projects under schedule pressure. It is not optional. The test section is the calibration check for the slurry machine and must be approved by the Engineer before full production begins.
- **Weather restriction at 60°F (vs. 50°F for HMA)** — slurry seal is more temperature-sensitive than HMA. Early morning paving in Hawaii's upland areas may fall below 60°F. Inspectors should have a thermometer.
- **Modified mix = new JMF submittal.** If the Contractor changes the aggregate source, emulsion grade, or proportioning, a new JMF is required before using the new mix.
- Slurry seal is measured and paid differently from HMA — check the individual contract for the payment unit (typically square yard).

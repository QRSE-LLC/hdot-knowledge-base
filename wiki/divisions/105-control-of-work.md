# 105 — Control of Work

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 105

---

## Summary

Section 105 defines who is in charge on an HDOT project and how the Engineer's authority flows down through inspectors and consultants. It establishes the submittal and shop drawing process (including timing, format, and the legal effect of acceptance), sets out the rules for interpreting conflicting contract documents, and establishes the Contractor's responsibility for survey/staking, quality of work, and subcontracting. The Inspector's limited authority is explicitly defined — inspectors cannot waive contract requirements or act as the Contractor's agent. The section also covers Value Engineering proposals and the strict rules on substituting listed subcontractors.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Engineer | Has all Director authority; makes final decisions on contract interpretation, material acceptability, and compensation |
| Resident Engineer | Representative of the Engineer with full Engineer authority on site |
| Inspector | Engineer's representative for inspection only — cannot alter contract or waive provisions |
| Shop Drawings | Contractor-prepared drawings, diagrams, and schedules illustrating how specific work will be constructed |
| Submittals | All items required by the contract to be submitted for Engineer review — includes shop drawings, material samples, color samples |
| Non-Conforming Work | Work that does not meet contract requirements; must be removed or remedied at no cost to State |
| Unauthorized Work | Work done outside approved work limits or without written authority — not compensable |
| Value Engineering Cost Proposal (VECP) | Contractor proposal to provide less costly items without impairing essential functions; cost savings shared with State |

## Spec References

- **105.01** — Authority of Engineer, Resident Engineer, Inspectors, and Consultants
- **105.02** — Submittals: Contractor must review before submitting; 6 copies required
- **105.03** — Shop Drawings: must be stamped/signed by Contractor; drawings requiring engineer stamp must be sealed by Hawaii PE; submit promptly
- **105.04** — Review and Acceptance Process: 30-day Engineer review cycle; rejection requires resubmittal within 15 days
- **105.05** — Conflicting documents: stricter requirement prevails; Contractor must report conflicts immediately
- **105.06** — Priority within drawings: numerical dimensions > scaled dimensions; larger scale > smaller scale
- **105.07** — Site examination: boring logs are informational only — no warranty of conditions
- **105.10** — Construction survey and staking: Contractor's responsibility with specific tolerances (Table 105.10-1)
- **105.11** — Inspection rights: Engineer may inspect at any point; Contractor bears cost of exposing non-conforming work
- **105.12** — Non-conforming and unauthorized work: Engineer may order removal; State may perform at Contractor's expense
- **105.15** — Value Engineering: contracts over $100,000 eligible; savings shared under HAR §3-132
- **105.16** — Subcontracts: Contractor must self-perform ≥30% of contract; no substitutions without written approval

## QRSE Notes

- **Inspector acceptance ≠ waiver of contract requirements.** This is frequently misunderstood in the field. An inspector who doesn't reject non-conforming work at the time does not constitute State acceptance — the Contractor is still on the hook at final inspection.
- **Shop drawing acceptance ≠ contract compliance.** The Engineer's acceptance "relates only to sufficiency and compliance with the intention of the contract" (105.04). The Contractor remains responsible for dimensions, fit, and conformance to drawings. A rejected submittal that was installed before acceptance creates a problem for the Contractor, not the State.
- **The 30-day shop drawing review clock starts on receipt.** If volume is unusually large, the Contractor can request priority ordering — use this proactively to avoid schedule impacts.
- Boring logs (105.07) are explicitly not contract documents — they are informational only with no warranty. This interacts directly with Differing Site Conditions claims under 104.08.
- **Subcontractor substitutions are nearly impossible** (105.16). Only 5 narrow grounds apply. Engineer written approval required. Getting this wrong exposes the State to claims.
- The 30% self-performance requirement applies to the total contract cost. Specialty items designated in the contract are exempt from this calculation.
- **Inspector stop-work authority chain (§105.01, §105.12):** An Inspector cannot independently halt work — they can only flag non-conformance. The correct sequence is: (1) Inspector observes a violation (e.g., HMA paving on a wet surface, §401.03(A)), (2) Inspector immediately notifies the Resident Engineer, (3) RE issues a stop-work direction under §105.12 as the Engineer's authorized representative. Work that is stopped without proper RE authority creates ambiguity about liability. Inspectors should always escalate rather than act unilaterally.

# 104 — Scope of Work

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 104

---

## Summary

Section 104 governs what work the Contractor is obligated to perform and how changes to that work are processed. It establishes the Engineer's authority to order changes at any time, defines the three mechanisms for changes (minor change, field order, change order), specifies the notice procedures a Contractor must follow to preserve claim rights, and creates the formal process for adjusting contract price and time. Section 104.08 — Differing Site Conditions — is one of the most litigated provisions on highway projects and is essential to understand for any project involving earthwork, utilities, or subsurface conditions. The notice deadlines in this section are hard cutoffs; failure to comply waives all rights.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Change | Any alteration to the work ordered by the Engineer; may affect scope, price, time, or all three |
| Minor Change | A change directed by the Engineer with no price or time impact; Contractor must protest within same-day if it believes otherwise |
| Field Order | Written Engineer directive to perform changed work — may or may not establish price/time adjustment |
| Contract Change Order (CCO) | Written order formalizing price and time adjustments for one or more field orders |
| Differing Site Condition (DSC) | Subsurface or latent physical conditions that differ materially from contract documents, OR unknown conditions of unusual nature; entitles Contractor to price/time adjustment |
| Methods of Price Adjustment | Six methods for pricing changes: fixed price, unit prices, proportionate lump sum, mutual agreement, force account, or Engineer-determined reasonable cost |
| Variation in Estimated Quantity | When actual quantity varies more than 15% from the contract estimate, either party may demand a price adjustment on the variance amount |

## QRSE Notes

- **The notice clock on DSC (104.08) is strict: verbal within 12 hours of discovery (or by 10 AM next working day), written within 5 days.** These are non-waivable conditions precedent. A contractor who fails to give timely notice loses the right to claim additional compensation — even if the condition was genuine.
- **No payment until CCO is issued (104.04).** Contractors sometimes proceed under an unsigned field order and expect retroactive payment. They won't get it. The CCO must be executed.
- Under 104.02(B), an oral notice must be given before the Contractor acts on a verbal directive — failing to give oral notice within the same day waives all claim rights for that directive.
- **Force account is the fallback payment method (104.06 method 5)** — at the Engineer's sole option. Know the Force Account procedures in 109.06 well.
- Quantity overruns >15% are common on earthwork, aggregate, and HMA items. Both State and Contractor can trigger the 104.07 adjustment — track this proactively.
- "The Contractor has the duty to determine means and methods" (104.01) — this language is important in disputes about construction methods or sequencing.

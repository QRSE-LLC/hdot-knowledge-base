# 101 — Terms, Abbreviations, and Definitions

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 101

---

## Summary

Section 101 establishes the language of every HDOT contract. It defines how the specifications are written (imperative mood means "the Contractor shall"), provides a reference list of all abbreviations used across the specs, and formally defines the key parties, documents, and concepts that appear throughout Divisions 100–700. Every other section in the specs uses these terms — understanding 101 is prerequisite to reading anything else. Of particular importance: the definitions of Engineer, Contractor, Contract Documents, Change Order, Field Order, Substantial Completion, and Working Day, which drive daily decision-making on every project.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Engineer | The Highway Administrator, Highways Division, HDOT, or authorized delegate — has full Director authority on contract matters |
| Contractor | Any legal entity executing work under the contract |
| Contract Documents | Includes contract, solicitation, addenda, notice to proceed, bonds, general and special provisions, specifications, drawings, change orders, field orders, and written interpretations |
| Contract Time | Number of calendar or working days for completion, starting from the notice to proceed date |
| Working Day | A calendar day when the Contractor can work 4+ hours with normal workforce, excluding weekends, State holidays, and Engineer-ordered suspensions |
| Change Order (CCO) | Written order signed by the Engineer adjusting price, time, or both — may settle claims or cover multiple field orders |
| Field Order | Written order from the Engineer directing a change — may establish, defer, or decline a price/time adjustment |
| Substantial Completion | Project status when all primary systems are functional and the facility can be used for its intended purpose; triggers punchlist period |
| Final Completion | Date all work is complete per contract documents |
| Liquidated Damages | Predetermined daily damages assessed for failure to complete work on time (see 108.08) |
| Notice to Proceed (NTP) | Written notice establishing the date contract time begins |
| Superintendent | Contractor's on-site agent responsible for all work and communications with the State |
| Surety | The bonding company guaranteeing the Contractor's performance |
| Punchlist | Engineer's written list of deficiencies to correct before final acceptance |
| Blue Book | "Rental Rate Blue Book for Construction Equipment" — reference for equipment rates in force account work |
| VECP | Value Engineering Cost Proposal — proposal to reduce costs while maintaining function |
| Float | Time between when an activity can start and must start; belongs to neither party exclusively |

## QRSE Notes

- The distinction between **Field Order** and **Change Order** is critical for claim tracking. Field orders direct work and may or may not set price/time. Change orders formalize adjustments. A contractor must protest a field order within 3 days or waive the right to claim additional compensation.
- **Working Day vs. Calendar Day** — check each contract for which basis is used. A contract on working days excludes weekends and bad weather days from the count; calendar day contracts do not.
- **Substantial Completion** triggers the punchlist period and starts the guarantee clock — make sure the date is documented in writing. Disputes about this date directly affect liquidated damages calculations.
- **Float** is explicitly shared property — neither the State nor the Contractor owns it. This matters in delay claims.
- The definition of "Contract Documents" is expansive — it includes **field orders and change orders**, meaning verbal directions that don't follow the notice protocol have no contractual weight.

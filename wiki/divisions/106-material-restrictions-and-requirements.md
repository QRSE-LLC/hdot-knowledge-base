# 106 — Material Restrictions and Requirements

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 106

---

## Summary

Section 106 governs what materials the Contractor must provide, how they must be sourced, tested, and approved, and what happens when materials don't conform. It establishes the material submittal and sample review process, sets the Certificate of Compliance requirements, restricts post-bid material substitutions to five specific circumstances, and mandates Buy America requirements for steel and iron on Federal-Aid projects. A key principle throughout: the Engineer's acceptance of a sample or submittal does not release the Contractor from responsibility for conformance — it only confirms the sample was reviewed. Materials in place are still subject to rejection.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Material Sample | Physical sample submitted to Engineer for testing; acceptance does not guarantee conformance of installed material |
| Certificate of Compliance | Document from manufacturer/supplier certifying material meets contract specifications; may substitute for or supplement sample submission |
| Non-Conforming Material | Material not meeting contract requirements; must be removed when directed, at Contractor's expense |
| Buy America | Federal requirement (23 CFR 635.410, 49 CFR 661) that steel and iron on Federal-Aid projects be American-made; de minimis exception for amounts ≤0.1% of contract cost or $2,500 |
| State-Furnished Material | Materials supplied by the State to the Contractor; Contractor receives at its risk and is responsible for storage, protection, and installation |
| Material Substitution | Post-bid change of specified material; allowed only under five defined circumstances with Engineer written approval |

## Spec References

- **106.01** — Source and quality requirements; all materials subject to inspection and testing at any time
- **106.02** — Material sources: Contractor may use materials found within project limits; removal requires written permission
- **106.03** — Unauthorized excavation: prohibited; site must be restored at no cost to State
- **106.04** — Material samples: Engineer acceptance ≠ final conformance; Engineer may retest at any time
- **106.05** — Sample submittals: 45-day review cycle; Contractor responsible for submitting at earliest possible date
- **106.06** — Source change notification: 10 days written notice required before changing material source
- **106.07** — Certificate of Compliance: format requirements and required content
- **106.08** — Non-conforming materials: Engineer may direct removal; if Contractor fails to comply, State performs at Contractor's expense
- **106.09** — State-furnished material: Contractor receives, stores, and installs at its own risk and cost
- **106.10** — Payment for deleted materials: canceled orders paid at cost + 7% markup; uncanceled material becomes State property
- **106.11** — Buy America: Federal-Aid projects require American steel and iron; de minimis exception applies
- **106.13** — Post-bid substitutions: allowed under 5 conditions only; savings accrue to State

## QRSE Notes

- **Engineer acceptance of a sample does not lock in conformance.** The spec explicitly states that "ACCEPTED" or "APPROVED" on a sample does not mean the installed material will be deemed conforming. The Engineer retains the right to test and reject at any time. This gives QRSE strong inspection authority.
- **Non-conforming material removal is mandatory when directed** — the Contractor cannot negotiate to leave it in place unless the Engineer agrees. If the Contractor doesn't act, the State can remove it and charge back the cost.
- **Buy America is a Federal requirement, not optional.** On Federal-Aid projects, non-compliant steel and iron = project non-conformance. The de minimis threshold is very low ($2,500 or 0.1%). Certifications of materials origin are required before incorporation.
- **The 45-day sample review clock** (106.05) is longer than the 30-day shop drawing clock. Track submittals and samples separately.
- **Material substitutions post-bid are very restricted.** The five allowed grounds are specific — unavailability, discontinuation, unsuitability, or manufacturer-provided equal at no cost. Any savings from an approved substitution go to the State, not the Contractor.
- When sources change (106.06), the 10-day notice requirement is often missed on projects with rolling material deliveries. Note this as a compliance risk on active projects.

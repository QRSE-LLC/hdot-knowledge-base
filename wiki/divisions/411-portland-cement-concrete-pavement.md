# 411 — Portland Cement Concrete Pavement

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 400 — Pavements
**Section:** 411

---

## Summary

Section 411 governs the construction of Portland Cement Concrete (PCC) pavement, with or without reinforcement, on a prepared surface. PCC pavement (also called rigid pavement) is used on high-traffic highway routes, intersections, and areas where long-term durability is prioritized over lower initial cost. It requires a comprehensive paving plan submittal before work begins, specialized equipment (finishing machines, vibrators, slip-form pavers), detailed joint construction, curing procedures, and rigorous surface quality checks. The minimum structural requirement is 14-day flexural strength of 650 psi. This section references Section 601 — Structural Concrete extensively for batching, mixing, and placement requirements.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| PCC Pavement | Portland Cement Concrete pavement — rigid pavement constructed from concrete rather than asphalt |
| Flexural Strength | Measure of concrete's resistance to bending; min 14-day value = 650 psi for HDOT PCC pavement |
| Paving Plan | Contractor submittal required before paving begins; includes mix design, construction method, equipment description, and joint locations |
| Slip-Form Paver | Self-propelled paver that places, consolidates, and finishes concrete in a single pass using traveling side forms; used for larger PCC pavement projects |
| Contraction Joint | Saw-cut joint inducing cracking at planned locations; controls random cracking |
| Construction Joint | Joint at the end of each day's pour or where paving resumes after a stop |
| Expansion Joint | Joint with compressible filler to allow thermal expansion without damage |
| Dowel Bars | Steel bars crossing transverse joints to transfer load while allowing movement |
| Curing | Process of maintaining moisture and temperature conditions to develop concrete strength |

## QRSE Notes

- **Paving plan must be accepted before any concrete is placed.** Unlike some submittals where the Contractor can proceed at risk, PCC pavement requires an accepted paving plan first. Any deviation from the accepted plan requires a revised plan before doing the deviant work.
- **The 650 psi 14-day flexural strength** is a minimum. HMA uses compressive strength; PCC pavement uses flexural (modulus of rupture) — a different test. Confirm the contractor's mix design uses the correct test method.
- **Joint layout is critical.** Misplaced or skipped joints lead to uncontrolled cracking. The paving plan must show joint locations; inspectors should verify joint cutting is done at the correct timing (before spontaneous cracking, typically 4–12 hours after placement).
- **Vibrators must not contact reinforcement or side forms.** Vibrator contact with reinforcement causes displacement and creates honeycombing around the bars. Inspectors should watch for this during placement.
- **PCC pavement is significantly more expensive than HMA** but has longer service life and lower maintenance costs. It is appropriate for high-traffic arterials, not typical on resurfacing projects.
- **Early use of concrete pavement** — if traffic must use the pavement before 14-day strength is achieved, the paving plan must address this with accelerated curing or high-early-strength mixes. This is a significant risk item; QRSE should flag early-use requirements in the contract documents.

# 415 — Cold Planing of Existing Pavement

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 400 — Pavements
**Section:** 415

---

## Summary

Section 415 governs removing existing pavement by cold planing (milling). Cold planing uses a rotary cutting drum to grind down the existing pavement surface to a specified depth, typically to remove a deteriorated surface course before resurfacing, correct cross-slope or profile issues, or reduce grade for overlay. Cold planing is paid on a lump sum basis. Key construction requirements include using equipment capable of producing a uniform surface without damaging the underlying structure, controlling drop-off heights for traffic safety, completing drainage provisions the same day as planing, and resurfacing within 3 calendar days of planing.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Cold Planing | Mechanical removal of existing asphalt pavement using a rotating cutting drum; also called milling |
| Cutting Drum | Rotating drum on the planing machine with carbide cutting teeth that grind the pavement surface |
| Drop-Off | Vertical pavement edge created by milling; creates a traffic hazard; limited to 3 inches max depth; must be tapered |
| Longitudinal Taper | Gradual ramp at the end of a milled section; maximum slope 6:1 |
| Transverse Taper | Ramp at the edge of a milled pass; maximum slope 48:1 |
| Mobile Reference | Device on the planing machine that averages grade over a minimum 30-foot length to control planing depth |

- **415.02** — Materials: None (no new materials incorporated)

- **415.03(B)** — Drainage: provide for drainage of planed surface on same day as planing

- **415.03(B)** — Surface tolerance: except at crown areas, max surface deviation 3/8 inch along a 10-foot straight edge (longitudinal and transverse)
- **415.03(B)** — Cleanup: clean and sweep per Section 310 — Brooming Off before opening to traffic; dispose of milled material per Section 201.03(F)

## QRSE Notes

- **Paid lump sum — no measurement.** If the actual quantity of milling is greater or less than anticipated, there is no automatic price adjustment. This matters if the scope of milling changes through a change order. Document the original lump sum scope carefully and process a CCO for any added or deleted planing.
- **The 3-calendar-day rule is a hard limit** — the planed surface must not be exposed to traffic for more than 3 days, and resurfacing must follow within 3 days. Hawaii's rainfall can damage a freshly milled surface quickly. Track milling completion dates carefully.
- **Drop-off management is a daily obligation.** The Contractor must construct tapers at the end of every working day for sections open to traffic. A missed taper overnight is a liability risk (vehicle damage, accident) and a contract default. Inspectors should check tapers before releasing traffic.
- **Damaged utilities during planing are the Contractor's cost.** If the Contractor damages a manhole, monument, or pipe while milling, they must repair or replace it at no additional cost to the State. Document the pre-planing condition of all utility appurtenances.
- **Milled material must be disposed of** per 201.03(F) — typically off-site. Contractors sometimes try to stockpile RAP on-site for their own use. Unless the contract specifically allows it, the material belongs to the State.
- **Dust suppression is required.** In Hawaii's communities, dust from milling operations generates complaints. Confirm the Contractor has adequate water supply and the shroud/spray system is operational before work begins.

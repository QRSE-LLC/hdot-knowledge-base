# 108 — Prosecution and Progress

**Type:** Division
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 108

---

## Summary

Section 108 governs how a project is mobilized, scheduled, monitored, and brought to completion. It covers the Notice to Proceed, preconstruction submittals, contract time calculation, allowed time extensions (and their limits), progress schedule requirements (TSLD format), weekly and bi-weekly meeting obligations, liquidated damages for late completion, suspension and termination procedures, and the pre-final/final inspection process. This is the operational backbone of a contract — it defines the rhythm of the project from NTP through final acceptance. The progress schedule requirements in 108.06 are particularly detailed and form the basis for delay analysis and time extension claims.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Notice to Proceed (NTP) | Written notice establishing the date work must begin and contract time starts; Contractor must start within 10 working days |
| Contract Time | Number of working days or calendar days allowed; begins at NTP date; ends at final acceptance |
| Time Scaled Logic Diagram (TSLD) | Required progress schedule format; submitted in SureTrak unless contract specifies otherwise |
| Critical Path | Longest sequence of activities that determines project completion date; time extensions only granted for delays affecting critical path |
| Float | Shared commodity — belongs to neither party; has no monetary value |
| Substantial Completion | Status when primary systems are functional and work is usable for intended purpose; triggers punchlist period |
| Liquidated Damages (LD) | Predetermined daily damages for failure to meet contract completion; assessed from missed completion date to final acceptance |
| Punchlist | List of deficiencies to correct after substantial completion; Contractor has until contract time to complete |
| Suspension of Work | Engineer-ordered halt to all or part of the work; Contractor may be reimbursed for direct costs in specified circumstances |
| Termination for Cause | State terminates contract for Contractor default; State takes over work at Contractor's and Surety's expense |
| Termination for Convenience | State terminates at its election; Contractor compensated for actual costs + 5% markup, no anticipated profit |

## QRSE Notes

- **The TSLD is the primary tool for delay analysis.** For claims involving time, the Contractor must demonstrate critical path impact using their own TSLD. Engineer's acceptance of a schedule does not validate the critical path or obligate the State to accommodate it.
- **Progress payment can be withheld for missing schedule updates** (108.06). This is a practical enforcement lever — use it if a Contractor falls behind on bi-weekly submittals.
- **The unauthorized lane closure fee** (108.09) — $500/increment, $5K/day — is separate from liquidated damages. It can be waived if factors were beyond Contractor's control.
- **Time extensions are only granted for critical path impacts.** Proving critical path impact requires a before/after TSLD analysis. Float is shared — if float absorbs a delay, no time extension is due.
- **Punchlist LD = 20% of primary LD rate.** This is often missed. If the primary LD rate is $2,500/day, punchlist LD is $500/day. Track the punchlist completion date separately from substantial completion.
- **Termination for convenience (108.12) compensation**: actual costs + 5% markup, no anticipated profit. Subcontractors get 10% on direct costs. The total cannot exceed the original contract price. This matters if QRSE ever needs to advise on State-initiated terminations.
- **The 1-year guarantee period (108.17) resets for items with discovered defects** — the whole affected scope restarts, not just the repaired item.

# Concept: Shop Drawings and Submittals

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 105.02–105.04, 106.05

---

## Summary

Shop drawings and submittals are the Contractor's formal documentation that proposed materials, equipment, and construction methods comply with the contract requirements. The submittal process runs in parallel with construction and serves as a quality gate — the Engineer reviews and either accepts or rejects each submittal. Critical principle: **Engineer acceptance of a submittal does not release the Contractor from responsibility for contract compliance.** If a Contractor installs work based on an accepted shop drawing that still fails to conform to the contract, the Contractor bears the cost of correction. Submittals must be managed proactively to avoid schedule impacts — the review cycle is 30 days for shop drawings and 45 days for material samples.

---

## Submittal Types

| Type | Description | Review Timeline |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Shop Drawings** | Contractor-prepared drawings, diagrams, and calculations showing how specific work will be built | 30 days from receipt |
| **Descriptive Sheets** | Brochures, catalogs, and product data illustrating materials and equipment | 30 days from receipt |
| **Material Samples** | Physical samples of proposed materials for inspection or testing | 45 days from receipt |
| **Color Samples** | Samples showing proposed colors for materials where color is specified | 45 days from receipt |
| **Certificate of Compliance** | Manufacturer/supplier certification that material meets specifications | As required by contract or Engineer |

---

## The Review Cycle

```
Contractor prepares submittal
    ↓ Contractor stamps/signs (shop drawings); indicates approval
    ↓ 6 copies submitted to Engineer
Engineer reviews (30 or 45 days)
    ↓ Accepted → Contractor proceeds
    ↓ Partially/fully rejected → Contractor must resubmit within 15 days
                    ↓ Review cycle restarts (30 or 45 days)
```

Each rejection resets the full review clock. If the submittal volume is unusually large, the Contractor can request a priority review order.

---

## Key Rules

**Submittal preparation:**
- Contractor must review and approve all submittals before submission
- Shop drawings must be stamped and signed by the Contractor — unsigned submittals are returned incomplete
- Shop drawings requiring PE stamps must be sealed by a Hawaii-licensed PE (not an out-of-state PE)
- Deviations from contract documents must be **clearly identified** in writing on the transmittal and on the drawing itself

**Engineer acceptance:**
- Acceptance relates only to "sufficiency and compliance with the intention of the contract" — not absolute conformance
- Acceptance does NOT relieve Contractor of responsibility for dimensions, fit, and field conditions
- Acceptance does NOT relieve Contractor of responsibility for deviations unless they were specifically identified and explicitly agreed to in writing with a price/time adjustment
- Engineer marks on returned submittals are NOT change orders — Contractor must follow 104.02 change notice procedures if it believes a mark constitutes a change

**Schedule:**
- Late submittal causing delay = Contractor's problem; no time extension for self-imposed submittal delays
- Contractor may proceed at risk before acceptance — State is not liable for correction costs
- Do not make changes to accepted shop drawings without written Engineer approval

---

## QRSE Notes

- **"Accepted" ≠ "correct."** This distinction is fundamental. Inspectors should not defer to accepted shop drawings as proof of conformance — inspect the actual work against the original contract documents.
- **PE stamp requirement is Hawaii-specific.** This comes up frequently with off-island contractors and engineering subs who may have their drawings sealed by mainland engineers. Verify the stamp is from a Hawaii-licensed PE.
- **The 30-day review clock begins on receipt.** If the Contractor submits on day 1 of a 90-day schedule and the review takes 30 days, they have 60 days left to build. If they submit on day 60, they may lose 30 days of schedule even if everything is accepted on first pass. QRSE should flag late submittal packages.
- **Deviation identification is the Contractor's burden.** If a Contractor slips in a deviation without flagging it and the submittal is accepted, the Contractor cannot later argue the State accepted the deviation. QRSE should review submittals for undisclosed deviations.
- **Material sample review at 45 days** is longer than shop drawings. For long-lead materials, delays in sample submission can cascade into delivery delays. Track material submittals on the schedule.

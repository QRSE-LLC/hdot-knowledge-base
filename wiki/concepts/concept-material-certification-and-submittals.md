# Concept: Material Certification and Submittals

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 106.05, 106.07, 106.08

---

## Summary

Every material incorporated into the work has to be documented as acceptable, and the form that documentation takes varies by material. Section 106.07 establishes the Certificate of Compliance; the Master Material Certification List translates that into a per-pay-item answer — whether a given material needs a certificate, a laboratory sample, an approved product listing, or designer approval, and which LABS office reviews it. Whatever the form, it reaches LABS under a JC transmittal. The Master Material List is explicit that it is a guide only: the standard plans, standard specifications, special provisions, and project plans all take precedence over it.

---

## Certification Types

| Code | Meaning | What it looks like in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **COC** | Certificate of Compliance | Manufacturer or supplier certifies conformance. For HWY-LB, **every COC requires test results attached** |
| **LAB** | Laboratory sample | Physical sample submitted for testing at the frequency in the Sampling and Testing Guide |
| **APL** | Approved Product List | Product appears on a pre-approved list; cite the APL number |
| **DA** | Designer approval | Designer reviews and approves, typically with manufacturer's specifications |

Many materials carry a combination, most commonly COC plus LAB, meaning both a certificate and periodic sampling.

---

## Reviewing Offices

The Master Material List assigns each item to the office that reviews the submittal. Matching the discipline correctly is what keeps a submittal from being rejected on routing.

| Office | Scope |
|---|---|
| **LB** | Bituminous — asphalt concrete, tack coat, emulsions, pavement markings |
| **LG** | Geotechnical — soils, aggregates, base and subbase, backfill, geotextiles |
| **LS** | Structural — concrete, cement, reinforcing steel, pipe, structural metals |
| **LR** | Other — conduits, conductors, signals, lighting, signs, planting |

---

## Forms and Routing

- All samples sent to the central lab go under the current **JC (job control) transmittal / sample card** form.
- Results from **State District Labs or independent labs** are forwarded to LABS under a JC form immediately after the Agency or its consultant receives them.
- Other documentation, including Certificates of Compliance, is sent under a JC form **at the time it is received** rather than batched at project end.
- The person listed under **Sampled By** must be a qualified sampler who took or directly observed the sample.
- Acceptability for every material entering the project is documented on the **Project Summary Schedule**.

## Acceptance Labs

Acceptance testing goes to the LABS central lab or a State District Lab. Alternatively it may go to an independent lab that is AASHTO certified for that specific test **at the facility performing it**, and that lab must be hired by the State, County, or their consultants rather than by the Contractor. Lab qualification requirements are in the Quality Assurance Manual, Section III, Laboratory Qualification Program.

---

## QRSE Notes

- The "send it when you get it" rule for certificates is routinely missed. A COC that surfaces during closeout is a finding even when the material was compliant.
- For bituminous items, a bare certificate without test results is incomplete. Check for the attachment before transmitting.
- An independent lab hired by the Contractor cannot perform acceptance testing. The hiring relationship matters as much as the certification.
- The Master Material List is organized by pay item, which makes it the fastest route from a bid schedule to a submittal plan at project start.
- Where the List and the contract disagree, the contract governs. The List says so in its own disclaimers.

# Concept: Methods of Price Adjustment

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 104.06, 109.05, 109.06

---

## Summary

Whenever the State orders changed, additional, or deleted work, or when a claim is settled, the resulting adjustment to contract price must be calculated using one of six methods defined in Section 104.06. The choice of method is negotiated between the Contractor and the Engineer — except for Method 5 (Force Account), which the Engineer may impose unilaterally at any time. The overhead and profit markups in Section 109.05 apply to all change pricing regardless of method. Understanding these methods is essential for QRSE when negotiating CCOs, reviewing change proposals, or documenting force account work.

---

## The Six Methods (104.06)

| # | Method | When Used |
|---|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Fixed price** agreed before work begins | Preferred; most common for straightforward scope additions |
| 2 | **Unit prices** from contract or newly negotiated | Best when quantities are measurable and predictable |
| 3 | **Proportionate unit price** for lump sum items | Engineer divides LS price by estimated quantity to derive a unit rate |
| 4 | **Mutual agreement** in any lawful manner | Catch-all for unusual situations |
| 5 | **Force account** per 109.06 | Engineer's sole option — no Contractor consent required |
| 6 | **Engineer-determined reasonable cost** + overhead/profit per HAR 3-123 | Used when Engineer disputes Contractor's proposal; based on GAAP |

---

## Overhead and Profit Limits (109.05)

These percentages apply to **all** change pricing regardless of method.

| Situation | Markup |
|-----------|--------|
| Contractor's own forces performing direct work | 15% on direct cost |
| Each subcontractor's own forces | 15% on that sub's direct cost |
| Contractor or sub passing work to a lower tier | 7% on amount paid to that tier |
| **Maximum total markup tiers** | 3 line items |

Example: GC → Sub 1 → Sub 2
- Sub 2 charges: $10,000 + 15% = $11,500
- Sub 1 passes through: $11,500 + 7% = $12,305
- GC passes through: $12,305 + 7% = $13,166
- No more than 3 tiers total; if Sub 2 uses its own forces the Sub 2 markup is 15%, but GC and Sub 1 pass-through are 7% each.

---

## Force Account (Method 5) Deep Dive

Force account is a day-labor pricing mechanism. The Contractor tracks all actual costs daily, and the State audits and approves.

**Allowable costs:**
- Labor wages + fringe benefits + 15% overhead/profit
- Materials at invoice cost + 15% overhead/profit
- Equipment at Blue Book rate (or actual shop rate if lower); no markup on equipment
- Subcontractor costs + 7% markup
- Insurance/taxes at actual cost + 6% markup
- Reimbursable expenses (pre-approved air/per diem for off-island workers)
- State excise tax and bond at actual cost (bond max 1%)

**What's NOT allowable:**
- Small tools (≤$500 purchase price)
- Equipment idle due to breakdown
- Overtime/per diem without prior written Engineer approval
- Pricing/negotiation costs

**Daily documentation required:**
- State Force Account Form signed by the Inspector each day
- Submit original + 2 copies with invoices and backup

## QRSE Notes

- **Method 5 is the State's enforcement tool.** When a Contractor refuses to negotiate a change at a reasonable price, the Engineer can impose force account. The Contractor must then work efficiently or the Engineer can stop the work and change methods.
- **The proportionate unit price method (Method 3)** is useful for lump sum contracts where a specific component is added or deleted. Divide the lump sum by the original quantity estimate to get the rate.
- **15% is the ceiling, not the floor.** In negotiations, the State should never start by offering 15% — that's the maximum. Overhead and profit can be lower by agreement.
- **Force account Blue Book cap**: total rental charges minus operating costs, over the life of the contract for a specific piece of equipment, cannot exceed the equipment's replacement cost. This prevents runaway rental claims on long-duration force account work.
- **Keep force account records concurrent with work.** An Inspector who lets the FA form fall behind by even a week creates a dispute. Daily signatures are a hard requirement.
- **"Full compensation" language in CCOs** (109.04): once a change order is executed, it covers all costs including overhead, profit, delay impacts, and insurance. The Contractor cannot come back later for "forgotten" costs related to that change.

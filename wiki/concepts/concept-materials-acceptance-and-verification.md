# Concept: Materials Acceptance and Verification Testing

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 106.01, 106.04, 106.05

---

## Summary

Acceptance testing is how the State determines that a material actually complies with the contract. The Standard Specifications establish the Engineer's right to sample and test any material at any time (106.04); the Sampling and Testing Guide for Acceptance and Verification supplies the missing quantity — how many samples, how often, how big, and taken where. The two documents have to be read together, because Section 106 never states a frequency and the Guide never states an authority. A separate and frequently confused mechanism, **verification testing**, applies only when the contract lets the Contractor's own quality-control data feed the acceptance decision. On federal-aid work that arrangement also requires FHWA approval. If a project is not set up that way, the verification column of the Guide does not apply at all.

---

## Acceptance vs. Verification

| | Acceptance testing | Verification testing |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Determine compliance with the contract | Validate the Contractor's QC data that is being used in the acceptance decision |
| **Who performs** | State, County, or their consultant | State, County, or their designated agent |
| **When it applies** | Always | **Only** when the contract specifies Contractor QC in the Materials Acceptance Program, and on federal-aid work only with FHWA approval |
| **Frequency** | Per the Guide's acceptance column | 1 to 1 for the first 5 QC tests, then not less than 10 percent of the acceptance frequency in the QA plan |
| **Guide reference** | Acceptance Sampling & Testing column | Verification Sampling & Testing column, plus Note 2 |

Two consequences of Note 2 that matter in the field:

- **The Contractor's QC frequency may not be less than the acceptance frequency.** QC does not get to be a lighter-touch version of acceptance.
- **Dissimilarity resets the schedule.** If a statistical comparison of the Contractor's QC data against the State's data shows the two disagree, verification reverts to 1 to 1. Rising verification frequency is a signal that QC data is drifting, and it is worth flagging early rather than at the end of a placement.

---

## Small Quantities

The Guide carries a separate column for small quantities, and the thresholds are material specific rather than universal. Common patterns:

- Asphalt concrete: under 150 tons per project, several aggregate tests drop to visual inspection or none; under 250 tons, binder and tack coat fall back to a Certificate of Compliance with test results.
- Concrete used for incidental construction: cement moves to Certificate of Compliance.
- Aggregate base and subbase: many entries carry no small-quantity relief at all and are marked N/A.

Read the actual row before granting relief. "It is a small job" is not a category the Guide recognizes on its own.

---

## Established Sources

Note 3 removes duplicate testing: a test is not required where the same source is already being tested on other projects, and existing results may be used wherever they apply. This is the established-source-with-acceptable-quality-control basis. It is a documentation question rather than a testing question — the results have to exist and be traceable to the source.

---

## QRSE Notes

- The most common error is quoting the acceptance frequency on a project that has contractor QC in the acceptance program, or quoting the verification column on a project that does not. Establish which arrangement the contract sets up before answering a frequency question.
- Verification frequency climbing back to 1 to 1 is a documented trigger, not a discretionary tightening. Treat it as an early warning on mix or placement consistency.
- Section 106.04 is explicit that acceptance or approval of a submitted sample does not change any contract requirement, and does not represent that the material as incorporated will conform. An approved sample is not an approved installation.
- Special Provisions can tighten any frequency in the Guide. Where they do, the Special Provisions govern.

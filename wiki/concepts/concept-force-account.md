# Concept: Force Account

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 109.06

---

## Summary

Force account is a method of compensating the Contractor for extra or changed work based on the actual, verified costs of labor, equipment, and materials — plus defined overhead and profit markups. It is used when the parties cannot agree on a fixed price for changed work, or when the Engineer elects to use it as the payment mechanism. The Engineer has the unilateral right to impose force account at any time (104.06 Method 5). Because payment depends entirely on daily documentation, force account work requires rigorous inspector oversight: unsigned forms = unverifiable costs = unpaid work.

---

## When Force Account Applies

- **Contract specifies it** — certain allowance items may be designated as force account from the outset
- **Engineer imposes it** — at the Engineer's sole option when no agreed price exists (104.06(5))
- **Claim pricing** — claims for monetary compensation cannot exceed what would be allowable under force account (107.15(D)(6)), though the Engineer may use other pricing methods

---

## Allowable Cost Components

| Category | Allowed | Not Allowed |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| **Labor** | Hourly wages + fringe benefits + 15% O&P | Overtime/per diem without prior written Engineer approval; travel to/from site without approval |
| **Materials** | Invoice cost + 15% O&P | Financing costs |
| **Equipment** | Blue Book rate or actual shop rate (lower of the two); no markup on equipment | Small tools (≤$500 purchase price); breakdown idle time; operating costs already in Blue Book rate |
| **Subcontractors** | Actual cost + 7% (per 109.05) | |
| **Insurance/Taxes** | Actual additional cost + 6% | |
| **Reimbursable expenses** | Pre-approved air/per diem for off-island workers; no markup | Insurance not reimbursable |
| **State excise tax** | At actual rate | |
| **Bond premium** | At actual rate, max 1% | Allowance items with bond already in contract |

## Equipment Rate Formula

```
Hourly Rate = [(Blue Book Monthly Rate ÷ 176) × Regional Adjustment Factor × Rate Adjustment Table Factor] + Hourly Operating Cost
```

- **Blue Book rate is the ceiling** — if Contractor's actual shop rate is lower, use that
- **Total rental charges** for a specific piece of equipment (minus operating costs) cannot exceed the equipment's **replacement cost** over the duration of the contract
- **Idle/standby rate** = 50% of computed Blue Book rate (when idle due to State-caused delays)

---

## Daily Documentation Requirements

**Every day force account work is performed:**
1. Contractor completes State's Force Account Form
2. Inspector **signs the form that day** (non-negotiable)
3. Contractor submits original + 2 copies with invoices and backup to Engineer

**Idle equipment (109.06(H)):**
- Contractor notifies Engineer in writing at start of standby period
- Submits a list every Monday of equipment idle the prior week (with dates, times, and reasons)
- Without these, standby pay is not due

---

## What the Engineer Can Do

- **Direct the Contractor to work more efficiently** — if the Contractor is working uneconomically, the Engineer can change means and methods
- **Reject work** — will not pay for unacceptable work or rework
- **Set replacement cost** if Contractor doesn't provide it

---

## QRSE Notes

- **The daily signature is the single most critical step.** An Inspector who fails to sign means the Contractor cannot prove those costs were authorized. This creates disputes and claims. Train inspectors on this explicitly.
- **Force account is the default fallback**, so QRSE staff need to know it well even if most changes are settled by fixed price. On contentious projects, the Engineer often imposes force account to force the issue.
- **"Actual cost" means invoiced cost.** The Contractor cannot mark up materials they took from their own stock to above their actual cost — and must certify stock origin in writing.
- **Subcontractor force account**: subcontractor costs flow through at actual + 7% to the GC. The GC cannot then add another 15% on top. The 3-tier markup cap (109.05) prevents pyramiding.
- **Monitor the replacement cost cap proactively.** On multi-month force account work, track running equipment rental charges. When the cap is approached, the Contractor must notify and get the Engineer's written agreement on the replacement cost.
- **Force account is not a blank check.** Contractor must still work efficiently and economically. The Engineer can direct a stop to uneconomical means, even during force account.

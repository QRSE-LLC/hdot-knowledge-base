# Concept: Sampler Qualification

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 106.04

---

## Summary

On HDOT and County federal-aid projects, samples must be taken or directly observed by a qualified sampler, and that person is named under **Sampled By** on the JC form. Qualification has two independent parts that are easy to conflate: a **certification** in the right material class, which lasts five years, and a **current qualification**, which lapses if the sampler has not passed an Independent Assurance evaluation or the certification exam within the last twelve months. A sampler can hold a valid five-year certification and still be unqualified to take a sample. Samples taken by an unqualified sampler are a documentation problem that surfaces at the worst possible time, because the underlying material may be long since buried.

---

## Certification

The Field Sampling and Testing Qualification Program (FSTQP) certifies in three subject areas:

- Asphalt
- Soils and Aggregates
- Concrete

**You must hold the certification matching what you are sampling.** Sampling aggregates requires the Soils and Aggregates certification; an Asphalt certification does not cover it.

- LABS offers certification testing in all three areas twice a year, in **March** and **September**.
- Certification is valid for **5 years**.
- For concrete, **ACI Concrete Field Testing Technician** certification is also acceptable, offered through the Cement and Concrete Products Industry of Hawaii (CCPI).

## Staying Qualified

Certification alone is not enough. Field samplers must keep qualifications current through annual **Independent Assurance (IA)** evaluations.

> If you have not either taken an IA evaluation or passed the FSTQP or certification exam in the last **12 months**, you are no longer qualified to take samples for Hawaii DOT or County federal-aid projects.

LABS administers IA evaluations four times a year. Exact dates vary, but the general schedule is by island:

| Month | Island |
|---|---|
| May | Maui |
| June | Hawaii |
| July | Oahu |
| October | Kauai |

Sign-ups for an IA evaluation on any island go through Wayne Kawahara at LABS.

---

## QRSE Notes

- Track certification expiry and IA currency as two separate dates per person. The five-year certificate is the one people remember; the twelve-month IA clock is the one that actually lapses.
- The island IA schedule means a missed evaluation can cost close to a year on a neighbor-island project. Plan around the window rather than reacting to it.
- Check qualification coverage against the work ahead, not the work behind. A crew certified in concrete cannot sample the aggregate base going in next week.
- "Directly observed" is a real option. A qualified sampler observing an unqualified person's sampling satisfies the requirement, and is often how a crew keeps coverage while staff work through certification.

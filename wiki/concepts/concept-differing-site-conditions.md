# Concept: Differing Site Conditions

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 104.08

---

## Summary

A Differing Site Condition (DSC) is a subsurface or latent physical condition at the project site that materially differs from either (1) what was indicated in the contract documents, or (2) what would normally be encountered in work of that type. DSCs entitle the Contractor to an equitable adjustment in contract price and/or contract time — but only if strict notice requirements are met. This is one of the most frequently disputed provisions on HDOT highway projects, particularly on earthwork, utility, and bridge foundation work in Hawaii where subsurface conditions can vary dramatically over short distances.

---

## The Two Types of DSC

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Type I** | Actual site conditions differ materially from what contract documents indicated | Boring log showed soil; actual conditions are decomposed lava rock |
| **Type II** | Unknown conditions of unusual nature differing from what is ordinarily encountered | Unanticipated archaeological remains causing work stoppage |

---

## Notice Requirements (Non-Waivable)

Both notice steps are **conditions precedent** — missing either one waives all DSC claim rights.

| Notice | Timing | Form |
|--------|--------|------|
| Verbal notice to Engineer | Within 12 hours of discovery **or** by 10:00 AM of the next working day (whichever is later) | Oral — can call the Resident Engineer |
| Written notice of potential claim | No later than 5 days after discovery | Written — deliver to Engineer |

The Engineer may extend these time limits in writing, but the Contractor cannot assume any extension exists without written confirmation.

---

## What Happens After Notice

1. **Engineer investigates** the site conditions promptly.
2. If conditions are found to materially differ and caused increased cost or time: **equitable adjustment** is made via change order.
3. Adjustment is priced per 104.06 (Methods of Price Adjustment) — most commonly force account or negotiated fixed price.
4. **No claim allowed after final payment** (104.08(C)).
5. **No claim if Contractor had pre-bid knowledge** of the condition (104.08(D)).

---

## Key Exclusions

- Contractor had actual knowledge before bidding → no DSC claim
- Condition could have been discovered by a reasonable pre-bid site inspection → no DSC
- Boring logs are informational only with no warranty (105.07) — but they can still support a Type I DSC claim if they showed different conditions
- Unknown contaminated or hazardous materials encountered → treated as DSC under 107.16(B)

---

## QRSE Notes

- **The 12-hour verbal notice clock is aggressive.** On active construction projects, subsurface surprises are often discovered mid-excavation. The moment conditions look unusual, the Contractor's team should be calling the RE. QRSE staff should confirm verbal notice was actually given if a DSC claim later appears.
- **Document contemporaneously.** The Contractor is required to maintain accurate, contemporaneous records once a potential claim notice is given (107.15(B)). QRSE inspectors should also document: conditions observed, date/time, who was notified, and any photos.
- **Type II DSCs are harder to prove.** The Contractor must show the condition is "unusual nature" AND different from what is "ordinarily encountered" in similar work. This is a high bar in Hawaii where variable subsurface conditions (basalt, ash layers, decomposed rock) are common.
- **The equitable adjustment is to cover actual increased cost, not anticipated savings** — the adjustment compensates the Contractor for the incremental difference between bid conditions and actual conditions, not for the full cost of the changed work.
- **If the Contractor proceeds without giving notice** — even if the condition was clearly a Type I DSC — they have no claim. Courts and boards of contract appeals have consistently denied DSC claims for failure to give timely notice.

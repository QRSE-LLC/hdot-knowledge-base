# Concept: Contract Change Orders

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 104.02–104.06

---

## Summary

A Contract Change Order (CCO) is the formal written instrument that modifies a construction contract — adjusting scope, price, contract time, or all three. On HDOT projects, changes flow through a defined hierarchy: verbal direction → field order → contract change order. No change is compensable until a CCO is issued. The CCO process is the central mechanism for managing scope changes, claim settlements, quantity adjustments, and extra work. Understanding the CCO process is essential for QRSE's contract administration work — both to ensure changes are properly authorized and to protect the State's interests during negotiations.

---

## The Change Process Hierarchy

```
Verbal/informal direction
    ↓ (must be protested same day if Contractor wants to claim)
Field Order
    ↓ (Contractor must protest within 3 days)
Contract Change Order
    ↓ (No payment until CCO issued)
Payment
```

---

## Types of Changes

| Type | Description | Protest Deadline |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| Minor Change | Engineer directive with no price/time impact (per Engineer's determination) | Same day oral notice, then written within 5 days if Contractor disputes |
| Field Order | Written directive to perform changed work; may establish, defer, or decline price/time adjustment | 3 days from receipt |
| Contract Change Order | Written order adjusting price/time; may cover multiple field orders | 3 days from receipt |

---

## What a CCO Can Cover

- Scope additions or deletions
- Price adjustments (increase or decrease)
- Time extensions or reductions
- Settlement of one or more field orders
- Resolution of Contractor claims (partial or full)
- Quantity variations (>15% triggers 104.07)

## Pricing a CCO: Methods of Price Adjustment (104.06)

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| 1 | Fixed price agreement before work begins |
| 2 | Unit prices from contract or newly agreed unit prices |
| 3 | Proportionate unit price for lump sum items |
| 4 | Mutual agreement (any lawful method) |
| 5 | Force account (Engineer's sole option per 109.06) |
| 6 | Engineer-determined reasonable cost + overhead/profit per HAR Chapter 3-123 |

## Critical Notice Rules

- **No claim without timely notice.** For verbal/informal directions: oral notice same day before acting, written within 5 days (104.02(B)).
- **No claim after final acceptance.** All claims must be submitted before final acceptance or within 180 days of the potential claim notice, whichever is earlier.
- **Protest a CCO within 3 days or you're bound.** Failure to file a written notice of potential claim within 3 days of receiving a CCO constitutes agreement with all its terms.
- **No pay without a CCO.** 104.04 is explicit: no payment for change work until the CCO is issued. This is frequently misunderstood.

---

## QRSE Notes

- **The CCO is the final word on scope, price, and time** — once signed by both parties (Contractor and Engineer), it settles those issues. Ensure QRSE reviews draft CCOs carefully before signature.
- **"No payment until CCO issued" is hard.** If a contractor performs change work under an unissued CCO and then demands payment, the State is not obligated. The CCO must be executed first (or the Contractor must have a field order and a pending CCO).
- **Watch for unbalanced CCOs.** Contractors sometimes negotiate inflated unit prices on add items while accepting deflated credits on deletes. The net may look fair but the individual items matter for quantity variation risks.
- **Force account is the fallback method at the Engineer's sole option.** When negotiations stall, force account is the enforcement mechanism. Make sure Inspectors know how to complete Force Account Forms and get daily signatures — without them, the documentation is incomplete and payment can be disputed.
- **CCO settlement of claims is final.** A CCO that says "full and final settlement of claim #X" bars any future claim on that item. Review settlement language carefully.

# Concept: Disputes and Claims

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 107.15

---

## Summary

Section 107.15 establishes the mandatory step-by-step process a Contractor must follow to make a valid claim against the State for additional money or time. The process is designed to funnel disputes through internal resolution before litigation, give the State the opportunity to investigate while conditions are still visible, and require contemporaneous documentation. Every step has a hard deadline — missing any one of them forfeits the Contractor's rights entirely. QRSE's role in this process is primarily on the State's side: verifying notice was given, directing records be kept, responding to claims, and advising on Engineer review responses.

---

## The Claims Process — Step by Step

| Step | Action | Deadline | Who |
|------|--------|----------|-----|
| 1 | Give **oral notice** of potential claim | By noon the day after the event (for informal directives per 104.02) or immediately upon discovering a DSC | Contractor |
| 2 | Give **written notice of potential claim** | Within 5 days of the event (varies by subsection — see below) | Contractor |
| 3 | **Maintain contemporaneous records** | Ongoing from notice date | Contractor |
| 4 | **Continue working** throughout dispute | Ongoing | Contractor |
| 5 | Submit formal **Claim** in writing | Within 180 days of potential claim notice OR before final acceptance (whichever is first) | Contractor |
| 6 | **Engineer reviews claim** | 60 days (<$50K) or 90 days (>$50K) | Engineer |
| 7 | **Appeal to Director** (if Engineer denies/underpays) | Within 30 days of Engineer's decision | Contractor |
| 8 | **Director decides** | Within 90 days of appeal | Director |
| 9 | **Court action** (if Director denies/underpays) | Within 6 months of Director's decision | Contractor |

## Notice Deadlines by Claim Type

| Event Type | Spec Section | Written Notice Deadline |
|------------|-------------|------------------------|
| Change / informal directive | 104.02 | 5 days from the directive |
| Field order protest | 104.03 | 3 days from receipt of FO |
| Contract change order protest | 104.04 | 3 days from receipt of CCO |
| Differing site condition | 104.08 | 5 days from discovery (verbal within 12 hrs) |
| Review/acceptance process | 105.04 | As part of submittal response |
| Sample submittal protest | 106.05 | With the submittal |
| Contract time dispute | 108.05 | 5 working days from delay event |

## What a Valid Claim Must Include (107.15(D))

1. Detailed description of facts and circumstances (dates, locations, affected work)
2. Contract provisions or laws supporting the claim
3. Copy of the related written notice of potential claim
4. Supporting documents
5. **If time extension sought:** specific days requested + critical path analysis + TSLD impact
6. **If money sought:** amount cannot exceed what would be allowable under force account (109.06); must break down into labor, materials, equipment, overhead/profit, other categories
7. **Certification:** signed by authorized Contractor representative under penalty of law for false claims

---

## Claim Amount Ceiling

A monetary claim cannot exceed what the Contractor would be entitled to under force account provisions (109.06). The Engineer may decide entitlement using any price adjustment method from 104.06, but the claim *submitted* cannot exceed the force account ceiling. This prevents Contractors from inflating claims with speculative damages.

---

## Subcontractor Claims (107.15(E))

- Sub submits claim to GC
- GC independently verifies the sub's claim
- GC limits its State claim to only portions for which the State is liable
- GC submits the sub's claim as a Contractor's claim

The State does not deal directly with subcontractors on contract claims.

## QRSE Notes

- **Every notice deadline is a condition precedent.** This is the most important thing to know about this process. No valid notice = no valid claim, period. QRSE should track whether contractors are giving proper notices and document when they fail to.
- **"Contractor must continue working"** (107.15(C) and 107.15(H)) — this prevents work stoppages as leverage. If a Contractor stops work over a disputed change, the State can terminate for default. QRSE staff should note work stoppages immediately.
- **The Engineer's 60/90-day clock** — if the Engineer fails to issue a written decision in time, the Contractor may treat it as a denial. In practice, QRSE should flag claims to the project team so the decision deadline is tracked.
- **Claims must be sequentially numbered** (107.15(D)) — a Contractor who files multiple unnumbered claims in a pile is not compliant with the procedure.
- **The 6-month window for court action** starts from the Director's decision date. This is the final exit from the administrative process.
- **Contractor certification under penalty of law** — false claim certifications expose the Contractor to debarment and civil liability. This is a meaningful deterrent to inflated claims.

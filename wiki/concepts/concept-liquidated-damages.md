# Concept: Liquidated Damages

**Type:** Concept
**Division:** Division 100 — General Provisions
**Section:** 108.08

---

## Summary

Liquidated damages (LDs) are predetermined daily assessments charged against a Contractor for failing to complete the work within the contract time. Because it is difficult to calculate the exact cost to the State from late completion (administrative costs, public inconvenience, traffic impacts, etc.), the contract sets a fixed daily rate in advance. LDs are not a penalty — they are a pre-agreed estimate of actual damages. On HDOT projects, two LD rates exist: the primary rate (failure to substantially complete) and a punchlist LD rate (20% of primary for failing to complete the punchlist after substantial completion).

---

## How LDs Work

```
Contract Completion Date  →  Actual Substantial Completion Date
         ↑                              ↑
     [contract sets                 [if late]
      this in writing]
         |______ LD period ____________|
                × daily rate = Total LDs assessed
```

LDs are deducted from monies due or that may become due to the Contractor — the State does not necessarily need to sue to collect.

---

## Two LD Rates (108.08)

| LD Type | Trigger | Rate |
|---------|---------|------|
| **Primary LD** | Failure to reach substantial completion by contract completion date | Set in contract special provisions (e.g., $500/day, $2,500/day — varies by project) |
| **Punchlist LD** | Failure to complete punchlist within contract time after substantial completion | **20% of the primary LD rate** |

---

## Key Provisions

- **LDs survive termination for cause** (108.11(C)) — if the State terminates the Contractor, LD accrual continues until the work is actually completed by the State or its replacement contractor.
- **LDs are waived between:**
  - Date of substantial completion → delivery of punchlist (Contractor can't be charged for Engineer's delay in issuing the list)
  - Date punchlist complete → date of final inspection
  - Date of final inspection → final acceptance
- **If a court finds LDs unenforceable** (e.g., determined to be a penalty rather than an estimate), the State may still recover **actual damages** for late completion (108.08(C)).
- **Unauthorized lane closure fees** (108.09) are separate from LDs — $500 per 1–15 minute increment, $5,000/day maximum.

---

## Time Extension and LDs

LDs are only properly assessed for delays that are the Contractor's fault. The Contractor can avoid LDs by obtaining approved time extensions for:
- State-caused changes (104.02)
- Permit delays not caused by Contractor (108.05(B)(2))
- Acts of God, weather, labor disputes, force majeure (108.05(B)(3))
- Material delivery delays beyond Contractor's control (108.05(B)(4))
- Suspension of work by Engineer's order (108.05(B)(5))

**Key rule:** Time extensions are only granted for delays affecting the **critical path**. Delays absorbed by float do not qualify.

---

## QRSE Notes

- **The LD rate is set in the Special Provisions** for each project — check the project-specific contract documents. The Standard Specs provide the framework; the rate is project-specific.
- **Document the substantial completion date carefully.** The LDs period ends on the day of substantial completion, not the day the Engineer agrees it was substantially complete. Disputes about this date are common. Get it in writing the same day.
- **Track time extensions proactively.** If the Contractor has a valid basis for a time extension and doesn't request it in writing within 5 working days of the delay event, they lose the right (108.05(B)). QRSE can note when delays are observed and prompt the Contractor to file — or note that they failed to.
- **LDs are a deduction, not a payment.** They come off the contract balance. If the contract is already mostly paid, LDs may need to be collected from the payment bond.
- **Punchlist LD at 20%** is often overlooked in final accounting. If primary LD is $2,500/day and the Contractor is 30 days late completing the punchlist, that's $15,000 in punchlist LDs on top of any primary LDs.
